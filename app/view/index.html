How the Women's World Cup was born

Women have played organised football for well over a century, but for decades the game was held back by bans and a lack of support. Unofficial world tournaments for women were played in Italy in 1970 and Mexico in 1971, showing that crowds and players were ready for a global competition.

FIFA tested the idea with an invitational tournament in China in 1988. Its success led to the first official FIFA Women's World Cup, played in China in 1991 with 12 teams. The United States won it, beating Norway 2-1 in the final.

Since then the tournament has been played every four years and has kept growing: 12 teams in 1991 and 1995, 16 from 1999 to 2011, 24 in 2015 and 2019, and 32 in 2023.

A short history

  • 1991, China. The first edition. The United States are the first world champions.
  • 1995, Sweden. Norway win the title and Germany reach the final for the first time.
  • 1999, United States. The final at the Rose Bowl draws a crowd of more than 90,000. The hosts beat China on penalties.
  • 2003, United States. Moved from China because of the SARS outbreak. Germany win their first title.
  • 2007, China. Germany retain the trophy. Brazil's Marta wins the Golden Ball and Golden Boot.
  • 2011, Germany. Japan beat the United States on penalties to become the first Asian world champions.
  • 2015, Canada. The United States win a third title with a 5-2 victory over Japan.
  • 2019, France. The United States defend their title, beating the Netherlands 2-0.
  • 2023, Australia and New Zealand. The first 32-team edition and the first held in two countries. Spain win their first title.
  • 2027, Brazil. The first Women's World Cup in South America.

Curiosities

Four-time champions. The United States have won the most titles: 1991, 1999, 2015 and 2019.

Marta's record. Brazil's Marta is the all-time top scorer at the Women's World Cup, with 17 goals.

Five champion nations. Only the United States, Germany, Norway, Japan and Spain have won the tournament so far.
